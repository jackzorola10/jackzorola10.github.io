Case study · Operations · Automation · Regulated supply chain

"Hire someone for this." I deleted the job instead.

At a regulated pharmaceutical distributor, matching stock received against supplier invoices took one person about four hours a day, and two managers asked for a new hire to do it. I took the task over, did it by hand until I understood it, and rebuilt it in three phases. It now takes a few minutes a day, and the hire was never needed.

Why it mattered

It wasn't a data-entry problem

Regulatory risk

By law, the supplier invoice is the source document for inventory. The pharmacy and the warehouse are separate licensed entities under one tax ID: logging stock to the wrong one is a compliance error, not a typo.

Cash risk

Every supplier has its own credit line and payment terms. Lose track and the account freezes. Then you can't buy medicines that only that supplier carries, and they may ask you to settle the full balance at once.

Headcount

Thousands of orders a month, and B2B orders with hundreds of lines. The Operations and Regulatory leads asked for a dedicated hire. Instead of approving it, I took the task myself to understand it from the inside.

How it got there

Three phases, two insights

Connect what already existed

I interviewed Ops, Purchasing and Finance. Suppliers already emailed invoice copies, an alerts channel already logged every stock entry, and a BI table already held the transactions. Nobody had connected them. Email routing, labels and Zapier fed a tracker, plus a supplier matrix with credit limits and terms.

4 h/day → 30 min/day. But not every supplier emailed, attachments broke, and discrepancies left no trail.

Go to the source of truth

Insight #1: every purchase legally requires a stamped e-invoice, so all of them can be downloaded from the tax authority's portal with the company's credentials. I moved to Airtable with AI extraction and bulk comparison against the BI export.

100% coverage, bulk matching, alerts. But AI was billed per cell: up to ~$215/month and rising with volume.

AI as the engineer, not the infrastructure

Insight #2: every Mexican invoice also exists as structured XML. There's nothing to "read". I used Claude to write the parsing and matching scripts, and plain scripts run them for almost nothing. I treated the AI bill as a trigger to re-architect, not as a cost to absorb.

3 min/day for an intern · $24/month. Unchanged six months later.

What I'd carry to any operation

  
Principles

  

    
Do it by hand before you automate it
The useful assets (email copies, an alerts channel, a BI table) only showed up once I did the job myself and asked three teams how they touched it.

    
Find the source the law already guarantees
Chasing suppliers for documents is fragile. The tax authority already holds every invoice, in a standard format.

    
Use AI to build the system, not to be the system
Per-document model costs scale with volume. A script written with AI's help scales for free. Cost is a design signal.

    
Machines suggest, people decide
Confidence thresholds, a review queue, and human decisions the matcher never overwrites. Trust is what makes people actually stop checking by hand.
