A new revenue line, built inside other people's hospitals

I was handed a business model in its infancy: pharmacies operated by us, inside partner hospitals. I turned it into a standalone revenue line across five hospitals, with up to four sites running at once. At the first site, results reached eight times what the hospital had achieved running it itself.

What I learned the hard way

A pharmacy in a hospital doesn't sell itself

Without active selling

Sales collapse

Stop the active selling effort and the rotating promotions, and revenue drops fast. Dispensing isn't selling.

Without traffic

It ends up in a corner

If the pharmacy isn't on the main patient flow, visible and with a reason to stop by, it gathers dust no matter how good the stock is.

So it's part of opening

Selling is in the gate

That's why visibility, a promotion calendar and operators trained to sell sit in the go-live gate above. They aren't an afterthought.

What I'd carry to any 0 → 1

Principles

Align the partner's incentive

A revenue share made the hospital want the pharmacy to sell. Rent would have made it indifferent.

Never send a customer away

The endless aisle turned "we don't have it" into a sale delivered to the branch.

Gate the opening

A short, explicit checklist beats a long plan. If one item is false, the site doesn't open.

Build the tool the floor needs

An online-first platform can't run a store. Shipping a point of sale was part of launching the business.
