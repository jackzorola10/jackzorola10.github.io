A "small" fee every payday, paying one card with another, "interest-free" months that cost you a discount. None of them feel like debt, and none of them advertise an annual rate. This calculator turns each one into the same number, so they can finally be compared.

Why it matters

Three ways a cost hides

Short periods

"Only 3%"

3% every two weeks isn't 3% a year. Compounded over 24 pay cycles it's more than 100%.

Wrong base

The fee is on what you process

When you pay a fee to get cash, the rate that matters is the fee divided by the cash you actually receive, plus the tax on the fee.

It never ends

Rolling isn't paying

A mechanism repeated every month isn't a one-off cost. It's a loan that never shrinks, paying interest forever.

How to read it

Principles

Compare rates, not payments

A low monthly payment can hide a very high rate. The effective annual rate is the only number that compares a payroll advance with a car loan.

Measure on the money you receive

Fees taken upfront, or charged on a bigger processed amount, raise the real rate. Always divide by the cash in your hand.

Count the tax

In Mexico VAT applies to commissions and to interest on most consumer credit. Leave it out and every rate looks better than it is.

Mechanisms before loans

The scariest balance isn't always the most expensive. Sort by annual rate and attack the mechanism you repeat every month first.

Educational tool, not financial advice. Real contracts can include insurance, fees or conditions this calculator doesn't model; for loans, compare against the CAT disclosed by the lender.
