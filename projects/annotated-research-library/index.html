Template + tool · Research · Critical thinking

A video proves someone said it. It doesn't prove it's true.

A method I built to keep what I read and watch from evaporating, and to always know whether an idea is a fact, a model, an opinion or a story. I use it to make decisions in my own projects. Every claim gets an ID and a type, contradictions get logged, and nothing becomes a conclusion until independent sources agree.

Why it exists

The default is to forget, or worse

Ideas evaporate

You watch something, feel you understood it, and three weeks later neither the link nor the idea is left.

Ideas lose their origin

When an idea does stick, it sticks without its source, so there's no way to tell a fact from someone's opinion on YouTube.

One confident voice decides

The most expensive mistake: changing a plan because of a single persuasive source that nobody checked.

The method

Every claim has a type

Captured

The original, verbatim, with URL and dates. It's never edited, so it can be re-read years later without your interpretation baked in.

Annotated

The central thesis, then each claim numbered and typed, plus a critical read: where it's strong, where it overreaches, what it leaves out.

Verified

Every data claim checked against its primary source, with the result written down whether it confirms or refutes.

Applied

Used in a synthesis or in a real decision, with links in both directions.

What I'd carry to any team

Principles

Cite, classify, question

Never rewrite someone's claim as if it were yours. Keep the ID, the type and your doubts next to it.

Reading and checking are different jobs

Critical reading finds weak arguments; verification finds wrong numbers, and sometimes clears a suspicion that was unfair. You need both.

Count sources, not citations

Two outlets quoting one study are one source. Confidence comes from the quality of the primary, not the number of echoes.

The library informs, it doesn't decide

When a source changes a decision, the change is written in the project, citing the claim ID. The library never overrides a decision by itself.
